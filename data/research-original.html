Research pillar

Secure and Sustainable AI Systems and Infrastructure

How can security and efficiency be evaluated together?

Published and preprint foundations

Resource-aware adversarial resilience

Investigating how adversarial activity affects the energy use and latency of sensing AI, and how defenses change those costs.

Goals

Measure attacks and defenses jointly across predictive utility, energy, and latency.

Intellectual merit

Connect robustness to operational cost rather than evaluating accuracy alone.

Selected publications

Sponge Attacks on Sensing AI ↗

Applications

Potential applications include evaluating attacks and defenses on wearable and edge sensing models, where battery life and response time matter.

Impact

The linked work studies energy–latency vulnerabilities and model-pruning defenses. Its potential benefit is a fuller basis for comparing security costs; this is not a claim of deployed protection or measured real-world savings.

Existing research

Sustainable machine learning

Studying methods for quantifying the environmental cost of machine learning and adversarial training.

Goals

Compare measurement choices and the resource implications of secure ML.

Intellectual merit

Make the tradeoffs between protection and environmental cost explicit.

Selected publications

Selected sustainability publications ↗

Applications

Measurement frameworks can inform experiments on the resource costs of training and defending AI models, including resource-constrained edge settings.

Impact

Published work reviews carbon-emission quantification and examines the costs of adversarial training. The intended benefit is more transparent tradeoff reporting, not a claim of verified emissions reductions in deployed systems.

Emerging direction · not an established deployment

AI infrastructure and agent systems

An emerging direction exploring resource-aware AI deployment, including future questions about data-center-scale systems and interacting agents.

Goals

Develop a research agenda for evaluating security, resource use, and evidence flow.

Intellectual merit

Study interactions between system-scale constraints and trustworthy AI behavior.

Applications

Proposed applications include evaluating security and resource use in interacting AI agents and AI infrastructure. Data-center-scale systems are a future research target, not an established lab deployment.

Impact

The intended impact is a framework for studying how security, resource constraints, and evidence flow interact at system scale. This remains an emerging agenda; no project-specific outcomes, infrastructure deployment, or operational savings are claimed.

Research pillar

AI Security, Privacy and Resilience

What makes intelligent systems vulnerable, and how can we protect them?

Existing research

Wearable AI across the lifecycle

Studying attacks and defenses in sensor-based activity recognition, including data manipulation and privacy risks.

Goals

Evaluate resilient learning and trustworthy interpretations of wearable signals.

Intellectual merit

Connect sensor structure, learning behavior, and security across collection, training, and use.

Selected publications

Adaptive and Robust Data Poisoning Detection and Sanitization ↗

Applications

Potential applications include assessing training-data integrity and resilient activity recognition in wearable and cyber-physical research systems.

Impact

The linked research investigates poisoning detection and sanitization in wearable IoT data. The potential benefit is more trustworthy sensing pipelines; clinical effectiveness, patient benefit, and production deployment have not been established here.

Existing research

Privacy in human–AI interaction

Examining how consumer AI writing and editing tools change the exposure of personal information.

Goals

Measure both protective capabilities and security risks in everyday AI tools.

Intellectual merit

Treat consumer tools as both possible privacy defenses and sources of new vulnerabilities.

Selected publications

No Innocence in Styling ↗

Applications

Findings can inform evaluations of privacy exposure and protection when people use consumer AI writing and editing assistants.

Impact

The published work examines both privacy-protection capabilities and security risks. It may help researchers and users assess tradeoffs, but does not establish universal privacy guarantees or measured adoption outcomes.

Existing research

Language models for cyber-physical systems

Investigating language-model integration with sensing, healthcare, and distributed decision-making.

Goals

Explore useful interaction while considering privacy, security, and resource constraints.

Intellectual merit

Connect language-based reasoning with the requirements of physical environments.

Selected publications

Wearable IoT data-poisoning detection ↗

Applications

Potential applications include language-assisted interpretation of sensor information and integrity checks for wearable or distributed cyber-physical research pipelines. Healthcare use is a prospective setting requiring separate validation.

Impact

The aim is to connect useful language-model capabilities with the privacy, security, and resource requirements of physical environments. The linked wearable-data study is supporting research, not evidence of clinical deployment or validated autonomous control.

Research pillar

Digital Forensics and AI Provenance

What evidence can we recover about digital content and AI interactions?

EMNLP 2026

On-device generative image-edit forensics

ConsumerGenEdit studies images edited using native consumer tools across devices and platforms.

Goals

Detect and localize edits, evaluate multilingual descriptions, and study compression effects.

Intellectual merit

Ground forensic evaluation in real consumer workflows rather than only fully synthetic images.

Selected publications

Edits in the Wild ↗

Applications

Potential users include researchers, journalists, and fact-checkers evaluating images modified by consumer generative-editing tools. These are possible applications, not stated partnerships or deployments.

Impact

The benchmark supports evaluation of edit detection and localization in realistic consumer workflows. Its potential benefit is stronger image-provenance assessment; forensic outputs alone do not prove an image’s truth, intent, or complete editing history.

Preprint

AI application forensics

Investigating artifacts left by the ChatGPT Windows application.

Goals

Identify and characterize digital traces relevant to forensic examination.

Intellectual merit

Extend application-forensic analysis to emerging AI interfaces.

Selected publications

Digital Forensic Investigation of the ChatGPT Windows Application ↗

Applications

Potential applications include forensic research and authorized investigations of artifacts left by AI desktop applications.

Impact

The linked preprint characterizes application traces that may support evidence analysis. Any operational use requires independent validation, lawful access, and appropriate evidence-handling procedures; investigative or legal outcomes are not claimed.
